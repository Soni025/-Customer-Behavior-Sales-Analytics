# Customer Behavior & Sales Analytics

An exploratory customer and sales analytics project focused on understanding purchasing patterns, subscriber contribution, and customer loyalty. The project analyses **3,900 customer purchase records** and translates customer-level data into business insights that can support sales strategy, customer engagement, and retention planning.

> **Business objective:** Understand who is purchasing, how different customer groups contribute to revenue, and how customer behavior can inform more effective business decisions.

---

## Executive Summary

The analysis focuses on three core questions:

1. How much revenue is generated by subscribers compared with other customers?
2. How is the customer base distributed across new, returning, and loyal customers?
3. How can these findings be communicated clearly to stakeholders?

### Key Findings

- **Subscribers contribute 45% of total revenue**, highlighting the importance of subscription customers to overall sales.
- The customer base is segmented into three behavioral tiers:
  - **New customers: 50%**
  - **Returning customers: 35%**
  - **Loyal customers: 15%**
- An **interactive Tableau dashboard** presents the analysis and makes the findings accessible to stakeholders.

These findings provide a starting point for understanding customer mix, revenue contribution, and opportunities to strengthen repeat purchasing and loyalty.

## Customer Segmentation

The analysis groups customers into three behavioral segments:

| Customer segment | Share of customer base | Business interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| New | 50% | Represents half of the customer base and is an important audience for first-purchase experience and engagement. |
| Returning | 35% | Customers who have made repeat purchases, representing an opportunity to strengthen ongoing engagement. |
| Loyal | 15% | A smaller customer segment that can be important to long-term customer relationships and retention. |

<details>
<summary>How to interpret the segmentation</summary>

The distribution shows that new customers form the largest segment, while returning and loyal customers together account for the other half of the customer base. This provides a useful basis for exploring how customers move from an initial purchase to repeat and loyal behavior.

## Business Implications

The findings suggest several areas worth exploring:

### 1. Convert new customers into returning customers
Since 50% of the customer base is new, businesses can investigate onboarding, first-purchase experience, and follow-up engagement to encourage repeat purchases.

### 2. Strengthen customer loyalty
Returning customers account for 35% of the base. Targeted engagement and loyalty initiatives could help strengthen these customer relationships.

### 3. Understand and retain subscribers
With subscribers contributing 45% of revenue, monitoring subscription engagement and customer experience may help protect an important revenue stream.

### 4. Use customer segments to guide decisions
Segment-level reporting can help stakeholders evaluate customer behavior and consider different engagement strategies for different groups.

These are potential strategic actions suggested by the findings, not measured outcomes of campaigns.
